# PAPER-DEV-01 — Discover papers and prepare permitted full text for cited research

Date: 2026-10-12  
Status: Partially implemented and offline-tested on 2026-10-12; pilot and human research-quality acceptance pending.  
Priority: Next focused research-paper increment requested by the user.  
Authority: [Roadmap section 43](../Homeopathy_AI_Product_Requirements_and_Roadmap.md#43-scholarly-discovery-and-permitted-full-text-acquisition--2026-10-12).

## User outcome and requirements

A student or researcher searches for homeopathy papers, saves references, sees which have usable full text, and researches selected reviewed papers with citations that reopen the saved original. Indian studies are discoverable without conflating Indian author affiliations with Indian study populations. Missing or blocked content remains visible and cannot become invented evidence.

Related roadmap work: DATA-01/04/05/06/08, PAPER-DEV-01/02/03, research services in section 26.0 and evidence/evaluation gates in sections 35/40/42/43. This single ticket owns the initial topic-search, deduplication, full-text resolution and bounded JATS-to-citation implementation. PAPER-DEV-02/03 remain expansion backlog, not duplicate tickets. It brings forward the required JATS portion of ING-04/05; ING-06 remains open. No promise of failproof retrieval or comprehensive literature coverage is acceptable.

## Implementation sequence

### A. Identity, persistent state and provider search

1. Inspect existing schema/jobs and extend their contracts. Reuse source/assets/revisions, permissions, publication, embeddings and `doi_references`; do not create a parallel paper evidence store. If DOI-required rows cannot represent PMID-only works, introduce a canonical scholarly-work/identifier relation with a migration and explicit justification. Existing DOI references must continue resolving; never create a fake DOI.
2. Persist work identifiers and provider-specific raw metadata snapshots with retrieval times. Normalize DOI, PMID and PMCID; exact verified identifiers deduplicate manifestations of the same report. Contradictory identifier mappings go to review. Title similarity only suggests a match. Preserve preprint, accepted manuscript, final article, correction and retraction relationships; multiple reports from one trial remain distinct publications linked to a study, never counted automatically as independent trials.
3. Persist search query, provider-native query, filters, date, result order, provider totals when available, cursors, retrieved count, cap and completion/failure reason. Search snapshots are time-bounded observations, not immutable evidence of exhaustive coverage. Fetch additional pages explicitly; do not imply the first page is the entire literature.
4. A shared adapter contract returns normalized records, provenance, continuation and typed errors. One provider outage does not suppress successful results from others; show partial search and individual failures. No silent widening of a selected provider, date range or source set.
5. Separate `study_country`, `author_affiliation_country` and `publication_country`. Study-country assignment requires saved methods/registry evidence plus reviewer status. Store unknown and multinational values. Search variants include homeopathy/homoeopathy and condition/design terms; India filters cannot depend only on title text.

### B. Durable resolution and bounded fetch

1. A selected acquisition job snapshots intended work, candidate locations and policy version. Resolve supplied PMCID/PMID/DOI, try eligible structured repositories, then DOI OA locations and supplied secondary-provider candidates. Deduplicate URLs/assets and cap attempts. Preserve accepted-manuscript versus published-version labels.
2. Keep independent axes: discovery (`complete/partial/failed`), access (`unknown/metadata_only/abstract_only/full_text_candidate/unavailable`), rights (`pending/approved/restricted`), processing (`queued/fetching/parsed/review_required/failed/cancelled`) and existing publication/index readiness. A successful download changes neither rights approval nor publication status.
3. Persist idempotency keys, worker leases/checkpoints, attempt count, next retry, typed reason and cancellation. Retries cannot create duplicate sources or publish a half-written asset. Use atomic asset/source linkage; clean up orphan temporary data safely. A crash after fetch can resume without another download when the validated saved asset exists.
4. Configure finite request, response-byte, decompressed-byte, redirect, document-count, page-count and elapsed-time caps. Proposed pilot defaults: 30 selected works, at most 5 candidate locations per work, 3 transient retries and a 30-second request timeout; retain existing stricter format limits. Record effective limits. PMC OAI runs serially within its documented rate/off-peak rules; every adapter has its own limiter. Honour `Retry-After`; jitter/backoff 429/5xx/network failures, and stop on permanent denial or malformed payload. Re-check current limits before enabling traffic.
5. Apply safe-fetch validation to every redirect/DNS resolution and every supplementary URL. Reject private/link-local destinations, invalid TLS, unsafe schemes, excessive compression, misleading MIME/magic bytes, login/error/CAPTCHA responses and wrong-article identities. A 200 HTML login page is not a PDF; a generic 403 is access denied, not proof of a paywall. No browser challenge bypass.
6. Persist rights evidence for the exact version: license text/URL, attribution, holder, checked time, reviewer and approved uses including storage, indexing, excerpts and model-provider transmission. A DOI, OA flag or free reading does not grant all these rights. Unknown/noncommercial restrictions hold commercial ingestion pending a valid permission basis. Metadata and abstracts also follow provider/content terms.

### C. JATS and shared evidence preparation

1. Add an explicit versioned JATS extractor, separate from the HTML detector and existing ALTO path. Disable external entity resolution and network access; bound nesting, entities, text size and parsing time. Handle ordinary article DOCTYPE declarations safely without fetching external DTDs. Malformed/unsupported XML fails visibly.
2. Preserve title, abstract, nested sections, paragraphs, lists, table headers/rows/footnotes, figure captions, acknowledgments/funding/conflicts and reference boundaries. Keep article metadata, references and cited works distinct from the authors' study findings. Image-only tables, unavailable figures/supplements and unsupported formulas are explicit coverage gaps, not silently reconstructed text. Acquire supplements only as separately validated/permissioned assets within caps.
3. Save immutable raw bytes/checksum, parser version and deterministic article/section/table/block locators. Store a mapping between exact normalized quote spans and original XML elements; byte offsets must identify their coordinate system. Tables retain the headers/units necessary to interpret cells. Never invent printed pages.
4. Render a sanitized inert saved-original view with section/table anchors and quote highlighting. The reader must not execute XML/HTML scripts or fetch remote content. Preserve downloadable originals subject to access rights. Citation reopening uses the saved revision even if the provider changes or disappears, subject to current access restrictions.
5. Route extracted content through existing text/coverage review, rights approval, publish and compatible-index preparation. Preserve study type, protocol/results status, population, intervention/preparation, comparator, follow-up and notice metadata without inventing missing values. Automated classification is a review suggestion, not proof of design or quality.
6. Chunk along section boundaries; keep tables and relevant headers together and use bounded context expansion. Apply all source/category/access/revision/index filters again after expansion. Bibliographic metadata and generated summaries are not original result evidence. Abstract-only sources carry this limitation through retrieval, answer and export.

### D. Find papers, source selection and research

Add Find papers alongside Ask. Result cards show title, authors/year, provider origins, DOI/PMID/PMCID, publication/study type, India-location basis, access/rights/processing state and correction/retraction status with last checked time. Unknown status must not appear as a clean bill of health. Citation count is optional discovery metadata, never a quality score.

Users can search and save references within existing account permissions. Only an authorized ingestion/review role can import into the shared corpus or approve rights/publication. Do not widen researcher roles to make the demo work. Import shows progress/reason/retry/cancel and opens existing source review; duplicate imports reopen the existing record. Search results and shared source evidence have separate access checks.

“Use in research” selects eligible ready sources. Empty selection remains empty; it must not fall back to all sources. Answers distinguish full text, abstract, protocol, trial, observational study, review and policy; cite study-specific passages and preserve null/negative findings and uncertainty. Findings from adjunctive treatment cannot be rewritten as evidence for replacing standard care. Risk-of-bias judgments need supporting methods passages and a stated framework; missing reporting stays unclear. Gap statements describe the searched corpus and search date, not universal absence.

Persist search provenance and evidence selection with the saved research. Later writing tools consume these same claim-to-passage links. Show acquired/reviewed/indexed counts separately from found publications and independent studies. Re-check correction/retraction status on import and explicit refresh; surface stale checks. Confirmed notices must trigger existing eligibility controls, preserve historical citation integrity and visibly flag affected saved research. Scheduled surveillance remains later scope.

## Research-level test questions

These are candidate prompts and review criteria, **not completed gold answers**. For each, a human reviewer must freeze eligible source revisions, exact supporting spans, required answer points, prohibited inferences, relevant passages and acceptable abstentions before a held-out run. Use roadmap 43.2 candidate IDs; replace a blocked paper only with a documented equivalent and update the case. Never generate the gold answer with the model being evaluated.

| ID | Research question | Required behavior / evidence |
|---|---|---|
| RQ-01 | In the Mumbai COVID-19 trial (PMC10650991), what were the population, intervention, comparator, primary outcome and follow-up? | Cited PICO and time points from methods; preserve standard care in both arms. |
| RQ-02 | For that trial, how many participants were randomized and analysed, why did these denominators differ, and what bias might follow? | Trace denominators to flow/results; separate reported exclusions from reviewer interpretation. Missing explanation stays missing. |
| RQ-03 | Do its primary-outcome results support replacing standard care with homeopathy? Explain the design's limits. | Correct adjunctive-treatment scope; no replacement claim; distinguish reported effects from causal confidence. |
| RQ-04 | What completed efficacy results does the ROCM protocol (PMC11966070) demonstrate? | Identify a protocol; planned outcomes/sample size are not observed effects. Seek a later report only with explicit additional discovery. |
| RQ-05 | Does the menstrual-irregularities report (PMC11659453) match its registered primary outcome and analysis plan? | Link exact registration/report versions and dates. If registry detail is unavailable, abstain from declaring concordance or outcome switching. |
| RQ-06 | Across the selected Indian trials, how were randomization, concealment, blinding and missing outcomes handled? | Per-study cited evidence matrix; distinguish “not reported” from “not performed”; do not confuse single/double blinding with proven low bias. |
| RQ-07 | Can the chronic-rhinosinusitis and hypertension trials be pooled to estimate one treatment effect? | Explain differences in conditions, interventions and outcomes; no automatic numerical pooling or fabricated effect sizes. |
| RQ-08 | What adverse events, withdrawals, funding and conflicts were reported across these studies? | Cite each field; silence is “not reported,” never “no adverse events” or “no conflicts.” |
| RQ-09 | Which selected studies were conducted in India, and which merely have Indian authors? | Site-based evidence with unknown/multinational cases; affiliations alone do not establish study location. |
| RQ-10 | What does the inaccessible IJRH paper show about long-term benefit? | Reference-only record cannot support a result. If an approved abstract exists, limit claims explicitly to it and identify missing follow-up details. |
| RQ-11 | What research gaps remain after this search, including null or inconclusive evidence? | Report providers/query/date/limits, distinguish no result from unavailable full text; no universal novelty claim. Include conflicting evidence when present. |
| RQ-12 | Two providers return the same trial and a separate protocol. How many publications and independent studies support the finding? | Deduplicate identical reports, retain protocol/report distinction, and count studies only when linkage is evidenced; uncertainty explicit. |
| RQ-13 | Reproduce a numerical result from a JATS table, including units, denominator and uncertainty interval. | Exact row/column and footnotes support all values; saved citation targets the table. Abstain if the table is image-only/unreadable. |
| RQ-14 | After a paper is disabled or a retraction is confirmed, answer the same question and reopen the earlier answer. | New answers exclude ineligible evidence; historical citation remains correctly identified and access-controlled with the notice, never silently replaced. |

## Acceptance and failure tests

- **Discovery:** fixture tests cover pagination, duplicates, conflicting IDs, missing DOI/abstract, provider-specific query syntax, cap reached, expired cursor, 429/timeout and partial provider failure. Record live smoke evidence for PubMed and Europe PMC; optional Semantic Scholar remains disabled/pending if credentials or terms prevent validation, without blocking the primary route.
- **Resolution:** fixtures cover permitted PMC JATS, repository PDF fallback, abstract-only, unknown rights, restrictive license, wrong article, HTML login body, redirected private address, denied access and no candidate. Every terminal state has an actionable reason; no fixture enters RAG through a bypass.
- **Parsing/citations:** nested sections, Unicode, tables/footnotes, references, ordinary safe DOCTYPE, malicious entity declarations, malformed/oversized XML and missing supplement fixtures. Verify quote-to-original mapping and reader reload, not just extracted character counts. Preserve existing PDF, HTML/TXT and ALTO behavior.
- **Recovery:** crash before/after asset commit, repeated import, lease expiry, cancel/retry, provider outage and parser-version update cannot duplicate evidence or activate partial revisions. Refresh cannot overwrite reviewed content or invalidate historical locators silently.
- **Authorization/eligibility:** metadata-only, draft, rights-pending, disabled, inaccessible, incompatible-index and superseded evidence cannot leak through search-to-Ask, context expansion, exports or reader access. Test selected-empty and user-owned reference isolation.
- **Pilot end-to-end:** up to 30 selected records, at least two permitted Indian full-text records (completed study plus protocol), plus reference-only and failed-access cases. Demonstrate search → saved reference → asset → review → publish → compatible index → research answer → exact saved citation after reload/provider unavailability. Pin rights evidence, parser/model versions and asset checksums; do not commit unauthorized full papers or secrets as fixtures.
- **Human quality:** run applicable RQ cases in Quick and Deep against the same reviewed corpus. Record retrieval Recall@10 ≥ 0.90, human claim support ≥ 0.95 and expected-point coverage ≥ 0.90 as inherited targets, not results. Report denominators and per-case failures; citation integrity, protocol/abstract handling and unsupported-answer gates must pass. The small pilot is diagnostic and cannot establish broad clinical/research validity. Missing human judgments or predeclared holdout scope means acceptance pending.
- **Cost/latency:** record calls, bytes, durations, retries and embedding/generation costs; set numerical run budgets before live evaluation. The previous CORE-02 paid budget is not renewed by this ticket. Offline synthetic tests and live-model quality are separate evidence.
